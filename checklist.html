Common mistakes that slow loans down

  1. Missing pages.Send every page of every statement, even the ones that say "this page intentionally left blank." Page 4 of 4 tells the underwriter nothing is missing.
  2. Unexplained deposits.Any deposit that isn't your regular paycheck or benefit will probably need a short letter and proof of where it came from. Large ones are the most likely to be questioned. Keep copies of checks and transfer receipts.
  3. Cash deposits.Cash can't be traced, so it often can't be counted. Don't deposit cash you've saved at home without talking to your loan officer first.
  4. Screenshots instead of statements.Download the official PDF statement. A screenshot or transaction list usually lacks your name, the account number and the bank's name.
  5. Moving money around.Each transfer between accounts creates another trail to document. Leave funds where they are until your loan officer tells you where they need to be.
  6. Opening new credit or making big purchases.A new car, furniture on credit or even a store card can change your debt-to-income ratio and credit score. Wait until after closing.
  7. Changing jobs mid-process.Your employment is checked again right before closing. If a change is coming, tell your loan officer right away.
  8. Outdated documents.Pay stubs and bank statements go stale after about 30 to 60 days. Expect to send updated ones if closing moves.
  9. Unsigned or incomplete forms.Tax returns, letters of explanation and gift letters need signatures and dates. Check every line before you send.
  10. Blurry photos or locked files.Scan in good light, keep pages straight, and remove passwords from PDFs before uploading them.

This is a general guide. Your lender may ask for more or fewer items depending on your situation and loan program. Check marks are saved only in this browser.
